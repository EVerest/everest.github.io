Ev15118¶

ISO 15118 EV-side (EVCC) implementation built on the libiso15118 ev::Controller.

Handwritten Documentation¶

Ev15118¶

EV-side ISO 15118 EVCC built on the libiso15118 ev::Controller. It provides the ISO15118_ev interface and drives a DC or AC session against a SECC such as Evse15118D20 or EvseV2G.

The v2g_messages notification identifies received responses for all three protocol generations, including PowerDeliveryRes before welding detection. It does not report transmitted requests.

Protocols¶

The SAP offer carries the protocols supported_protocols lists, a comma-separated list of ISO15118_20, ISO15118_2 and DIN70121 in priority order: the first entry is offered with priority 1. For example ISO15118_2,ISO15118_20 prefers ISO 15118-2 over ISO 15118-20. The list is empty unless set, so a configuration offers only the protocols it names. The module fails at startup when the list is empty, names an unknown protocol, or names one twice.

  • ISO 15118-20 (AC, AC BPT, AC DER IEC, AC DER SAE, DC, DC BPT), EIM, plaintext or TLS 1.3 with a client certificate.

  • ISO 15118-2 (AC and DC), EIM or Plug & Charge (Contract). PnC needs TLS [V2G2-632]: with enable_pnc and TLS the EV offers PnC and EIM, and presents the configured contract certificate in PaymentDetails; without a contract certificate (or with pnc_force_cert_install) it runs a CertificateInstallation with the OEM provisioning certificate first, if the SECC offers installation in ServiceDetail for ServiceID 2 (ParameterSetID 1). A FAILED ServiceDetail response stops the session. If a successful response does not offer installation, it uses an available contract, or EIM if offered, and stops if neither is usable. An enforced Contract payment option remains selected without a certificate. TLS 1.2 is used for -2; a tls_version pinned to "1.3" runs it over TLS 1.3 anyway, and the module warns at startup about the deviation. An installed contract is kept in pnc_contract_store_dir when that is set, and later sessions present it instead of the configured contract.

  • DIN SPEC 70121 (DC, plaintext only), no authorization step.

The ISO 15118-2 / DIN EVCCID is the 6 byte MAC of the resolved device (the evcc_id string is ISO 15118-20 only). The EnergyTransferMode of start_charging selects the -2 / DIN transfer mode; the AC values are taken from the iso2_ac_* options. A BPT or DER mode has no -2 / DIN equivalent and degrades to the matching unidirectional mode, with a warning when the pre-20 generation is offered at all.

Which payment option the EV picks is config driven, because the EvManager default payment option is auto: with enable_pnc and TLS the EV prefers Contract whenever the SECC offers it, and the -2 engine falls back to EIM when it does not. SelectedPaymentOption only overrides that per session; an explicit ExternalPayment turns the preference off for that session, an explicit Contract keeps it on. enforce_payment_option makes the EV select Contract even when the SECC does not offer it (negative testing). Without enable_pnc the EV offers EIM only.

The session runs over plain TCP or TLS (tls / tls_version, see Transport security). The AC DER IEC and AC DER SAE services are also negotiated (assuming a three-phase inverter relay). The EV negotiates DER functions but never executes one; it publishes what was negotiated and received (see Published DER variables).

Bidirectional power transfer (BPT) is negotiated for both AC_BPT and DC_BPT in Dynamic control mode. The advertised discharge limits come from the *_discharge_* config settings; set_bpt_dc_params overrides the configured DC discharge power and current. Like set_dc_params it states vehicle limits: they reach a running DC_BPT or MCS_BPT session at once and stay for every later BPT session until the next set_bpt_dc_params. A field it leaves out keeps its previous value. During a DC_BPT or MCS_BPT session the update is validated with the session’s parameters and rejected whole when invalid. These settings are magnitudes: the EV sends discharge limits, var injection limits and excited discharge powers negated, as ISO 15118-20 [V2G20-1035] requires. AC_BPT assumes a three-phase inverter relay. Reverse power flow itself is driven entirely by the SECC’s target-power directives; the EVSE’s advertised discharge limits are logged for visibility.

Transport security¶

tls picks whether TLS runs: off requests no TLS in SDP, prefer requests it and accepts a plaintext SECC, require rejects an SDP response without TLS. off runs ISO 15118-20 and ISO 15118-2 over plain TCP; for ISO 15118-20 this deviates from [V2G20-2677] and is meant for testing (the config-sil-*-d20-plaintext.yaml configurations use it), and Plug & Charge over plain TCP additionally needs allow_pnc_without_tls. tls_version picks the version when TLS runs:

  • auto offers TLS 1.3, with the vehicle chain loaded for mutual TLS, when the SAP offer holds ISO 15118-20, and TLS 1.2 when it holds ISO 15118-2. An explicit advertised_app_protocols list counts as the offer. When the vehicle chain or key is not configured, missing or unreadable at connect, 1.3 is dropped and the EV logs why, once per reason. When no version is left start_charging is rejected with an error naming the enabled protocols and why 1.3 is unavailable; a chain file found unusable only at connect stops the session there with the same error. After the handshake the SAP offer is rebuilt for the negotiated version: only ISO 15118-20 over TLS 1.3, only ISO 15118-2 over TLS 1.2. When no enabled protocol is left the EV closes the connection and stops with an error naming the version and the enabled protocols; it never falls back to plaintext. An explicit advertised_app_protocols list is never narrowed after the handshake.

  • "1.2" and "1.3" pin that version and offer every enabled protocol over it. ISO 15118-2 over TLS 1.3 and ISO 15118-20 over TLS 1.2 deviate from the standards and are named in a startup warning. A pinned "1.3" offers TLS 1.3 even without a vehicle chain.

DIN SPEC 70121 is never offered over TLS.

Default

Description

device

auto

Ethernet device used for high-level communication. auto picks the first interface with an IPv6 link-local address, and the module fails at startup when there is none. A named device that does not resolve at startup is kept with a warning; start_charging returns false while it is missing.

02:00:00:00:00:01

EVCC identifier sent to the SECC, 1 to 255 characters. A MAC address and a vehicle identifier (WMIV1234567890ABCDEX) are both valid.

0

Response watchdog timeout in milliseconds. 0 uses the per-message ISO 15118 timeouts from the specification table.

0

Bound on the ISO 15118-20 authorization loop. The EV re-sends AuthorizationReq while the EVSE reports Ongoing and after a declined attempt, so the driver can authorize again, and stops the session once this bound expires. 0 uses V2G_EVCC_Ongoing_Timeout (60000 ms).

Dynamic

Preferred charge-loop control mode, Scheduled or Dynamic. ISO 15118-20 only; ISO 15118-2 and DIN SPEC 70121 always run scheduled.

pnc_oem_prov_key_password_path

""

pnc_force_cert_install

false

Run a CertificateInstallation even when a contract certificate is configured. Requires the SECC to offer the Certificate service and its installation parameter set in ServiceDetail.

pnc_contract_store_dir

""

Directory that keeps an installed contract as CONTRACT_CHAIN.pem (leaf first), CONTRACT_LEAF.key and EMAID.txt, each written to a temporary file of mode 0600 in the directory and renamed into place. Every session that can offer PnC reads it before starting and presents the stored contract when all three files are present, the key belongs to the leaf and the eMAID matches the leaf; otherwise it uses the configured contract material, with a warning unless the directory holds none of the files yet. An installed contract whose eMAID does not match its leaf is not stored. A failed store is logged and only the installing session, and its resume after a pause, presents the contract. The directory is synced after the renames; a failed sync is logged as a warning and the contract stays installed. Empty disables persistence. Give each Ev15118 instance its own directory; two instances sharing one can mix their files.

DER control function negotiation¶

For the AC DER IEC service the EV declares which IEC DER control functions it supports through the der_* options above; these form a bitmask carried in the ServiceDetail exchange. Among the SECC’s Dynamic parameter sets the EV selects the first whose declared functions are a subset of the supported set (a set with no DERControlFunctions parameter counts as compatible). A mask carrying bits above the functions the EV models names functions it cannot honor, so such a set is not a subset even when its remaining bits are supported. When no offered set fits, der_stop_on_unsupported_functions decides the outcome: true stops the session cleanly, while false proceeds with the first Dynamic set and warns about the unsupported functions. At runtime, a DSO setpoint (Q or cos phi) that arrives in a charge-loop response without the matching function having been negotiated is dropped before the directive is surfaced; all other fields pass through unchanged.

For the AC DER SAE service (ISO 15118-20 AMD1 Annex M) the EV describes its inverter in the ChargeParameterDiscovery request; the SECC enables functions in its responses and the EV acknowledges them.

SAE inverter profile¶

The file named by sae_inverter_profile_path is one JSON object. Every key is optional and keeps its built-in default when absent; an unknown or repeated key, a wrong JSON type, an out-of-range number or an illegal enum string fails the parse. The parsed profile, absent keys at their defaults, is then checked with libiso15118’s validate_sae_profile (voltage window, frequency, power factors, identity lengths, supported modes and totals); a problem fails the module at startup, naming the key. Powers, var, VA and siemens values are totals across ac_phase_count lines.

  • Identity strings, at most 32 bytes: inverter_sw_version, inverter_hw_version (absent by default), inverter_manufacturer, inverter_model, inverter_serial_number.

  • supported_modes: array of SAE function names, which must include "charge" and "discharge". The names are those of libiso15118’s sae::SAE_FUNCTION_NAMES, one per AMD1 Table M.6 bit: charge, discharge, enter service, constant power factor under excited, constant power factor over excited, constant var, constant watt, frequency droop, over frequency may trip curve, over frequency must trip curve, over voltage may trip curve, over voltage momentary cessation trip curve, over voltage must trip curve, under frequency may trip curve, under frequency must trip curve, under voltage may trip curve, under voltage momentary cessation trip curve, under voltage must trip curve, limit maximum discharge power, evse target reactive power, evse target active power, volt var, volt watt and watt var.

  • Numbers, unit in the key suffix: max_apparent_power_{charging,discharging}_var_{absorption,injection}_va, max_var_{absorption,injection}_{charging,discharging}_var, reactive_susceptance_s, over_excited_discharge_power_w, under_excited_discharge_power_w, nominal_voltage_v, maximum_voltage_v, minimum_voltage_v, nominal_voltage_offset_v, nominal_frequency_hz; and the ratios over_excited_power_factor and under_excited_power_factor, in (0, 1].

  • Unsigned integers: useable_watt_hours, minimum_charging_duration_s, duration_maximum_charge_rate_s, duration_maximum_discharge_rate_s, and j3072_certification_date in seconds since the Unix epoch.

  • j3072_certified: boolean.

  • Enum strings: ieee1547_normal_category (CategoryA, CategoryB), ieee1547_abnormal_category (CategoryI, CategoryII, CategoryIII), operational_state (On, Off), connection_status (Connected, Disconnected).

Published DER variables¶

  • der_negotiated_functions: the negotiated functions, with the flavor (AC_DER_IEC or AC_DER_SAE). For AC_DER_IEC this is the demand of the selected parameter set in iec_functions (DerIecFunction), published once from ServiceDetail; without der_stop_on_unsupported_functions the fallback set can demand functions the EV does not support. For AC_DER_SAE it is the enabled modes the EV echoes in sae_functions (DerSaeFunction), published every ChargeParameterDiscovery round and when the charge loop changes the set. The flavor is the annex the session runs, whatever energy mode start_charging asked for. A resume continues the retained session at ChargeParameterDiscovery: a resumed AC_DER_SAE session publishes its enabled modes again, and a resumed AC_DER_IEC session, which skips ServiceDetail, publishes nothing and keeps the paused session’s value.

  • der_control_received: a summary of each DER control block the SECC sends, with the flavor and the source (ChargeParameterDiscovery or ChargeLoop). SAE blocks carry permit_service, enabled_functions (DerSaeFunction) and any problems. enabled_functions lists what the EV has enabled after the block, limited to the functions it supports, the set it echoes next; a charge loop block omits the functions it does not update and those stay as they were. A block that enables functions the EV does not support names them in problems, which never stops the session. A block that stops the session under der_stop_on_invalid_control is still published, with its problems and the set the EV last held, before the session ends. IEC Dynamic charge loop blocks carry the DSO Q and cos phi setpoints when present; the first of a session is published, and a later one only when its setpoints change. IEC Scheduled charge loop blocks are not published.

  • ac_evse_target_power: the Dynamic charge loop targets, for AC_DER_IEC and AC_DER_SAE as for plain AC. AC_DER_IEC in Scheduled mode does not publish ac_evse_target_power.

update_present_values also takes present_frequency in Hz and der_alarm_status (AMD1 Table M.9), both for AC_DER_SAE, where the voltage is the measured grid voltage. start_charging clears the voltage, active power, frequency and DER alarm status, so values reported before it are discarded; a voltage or frequency not reported since falls back to the profile’s nominal value.

Departure time and requested energy¶

start_charging takes DepartureTime in seconds from the call and EAmount in kWh. Both must be finite and not negative; 0 states nothing.

An EAmount above 0 is the session’s energy request, in Wh on the wire: the ISO 15118-2 AC EAmount, the ISO 15118-2 DC and DIN SPEC 70121 EVEnergyRequest, the ISO 15118-20 Dynamic ScheduleExchange and Dynamic charge loop target and maximum energy, and the Scheduled ScheduleExchange EVTargetEnergyRequest. The minimum energy is always 0. With EAmount 0, optional fields are left out and mandatory ones fall back: ISO 15118-2 AC to iso2_ac_e_amount_wh, the ISO 15118-20 Dynamic fields to the energy_capacity of set_dc_params, whatever the energy service. ISO 15118-2 caps EAmount and EVEnergyRequest at 200000 Wh ([V2G2-832]), the configured fallback included; DIN SPEC 70121 caps EVEnergyRequest at 32767000 Wh, the largest value its PhysicalValueType carries. A capped value is sent at the maximum and logged as a warning once per session. ISO 15118-20 has no cap.

A DepartureTime above 0 is sent as the seconds left until departure, counting down, in the ISO 15118-2 AC and DC ChargeParameterDiscovery, the ISO 15118-20 ScheduleExchange of either control mode and the ISO 15118-20 Dynamic charge loops. ISO 15118-2 sends 0 once the departure has passed. ISO 15118-20 requires a DepartureTime above 0 ([V2G20-2103]), so a departure that is not stated or has passed is left out where optional, and the mandatory Dynamic ScheduleExchange DepartureTime is sent as 4294967295, no deadline.

Limitations¶

The implementation has a deliberately narrow scope:

  • The requested energy is static. The energy request stays what start_charging stated for the whole session; energy already delivered is not subtracted from it (see Departure time and requested energy).

  • DC, MCS and AC only. start_charging accepts DC, DC BPT, MCS, MCS BPT, AC single/three-phase, AC BPT, AC DER IEC and AC DER SAE energy-transfer modes; WPT sessions are not supported. MCS is the megawatt DC service of ISO 15118-20: the same DC parameter discovery, cable check, pre-charge and charge loop under a different service id, so it rides the DC states and has no ISO 15118-2 or DIN equivalent.

  • DER functions are never executed. Received DER control blocks, except IEC Scheduled ones, are published as summaries; acting on them is the consumer’s job. DER curves dictated in DER_AC_ChargeParameterDiscoveryRes are logged, and the summary carries none of their values. The three-phase inverter relay use case is assumed.

  • AC DER SAE is Dynamic only. A preferred Scheduled control mode is ignored for that service.

  • Plug & Charge is ISO 15118-2 only. ISO 15118-20 authorization is EIM only. An installed contract is persisted only to pnc_contract_store_dir, not to EvseSecurity.

  • Dynamic BPT only. BPT is negotiated in Dynamic control mode; reverse power flow follows SECC targets. SAE J2847/2 (enable_sae_j2847_v2g_v2h) is not implemented.

Threading¶

A single worker thread, started from ready(), runs one V2G session at a time. start_charging hands a request to the worker; the worker constructs the ev::Controller and runs its event loop until the session ends, then waits for the next request. Session state is guarded by a monitor so command handlers and the worker coordinate safely, including during module teardown.

start_charging returns false, and starts nothing, when the worker is not running, a session is active, the energy transfer mode is unsupported, the HLC interface is missing, or the session configuration is invalid. An invalid configuration includes a protocol offer left empty by the requested service or transport, for example DIN SPEC 70121 alone for an AC mode or over TLS, and a DC or MCS mode before set_dc_params gave a positive maximum power, current and voltage.

set_dc_params merges the fields it carries into the stored values; a field it leaves out keeps its previous value. The merged values are validated as a whole: an invalid update is logged and rejected, and neither stored nor pushed to a running session. set_fault is not supported and logs a warning. A session that ends publishes ev_power_ready false before v2g_session_finished. A session that fails to start after start_charging returned true still ends with v2g_session_finished.

pause_charging ends the session with SessionStop(Pause); the paused session is stored and handed to the next ev::Controller as ev::config::EvConfig::resume. stop_charging and abort_charging discard it. A stop from either side outranks a pause: when the charger terminates while a pause is pending, or stop_charging arrives before the paused SessionStop is sent, the session terminates and nothing is kept for a resume. In ISO 15118-20 a pause requested before PowerDelivery(Start) terminates the session as well [V2G20-2644]. cp_state_changed is latched and replayed to every new controller, and the first report switches the CP-dependent checks (the DC cable check) on. E or F during a session terminates it without SessionStop, as abort_charging does [V2G2-737], and cancels a session that was accepted but has not started yet.

For ISO 15118-2 (AC and DC), DIN SPEC 70121 (DC), and ISO 15118-20 (DC and MCS), an accepted PowerDelivery(Stop) response publishes power_delivery_stopped before WeldingDetection or SessionStop. The EV may then open its contactor and set CP B while V2G shutdown continues. ISO 15118-20 AC does not publish this event.

For ISO 15118-2 AC, ac_evse_max_current publishes the EVSEMaxCurrent offered in ChargeParameterDiscovery and reported in each ChargingStatus response that carries it. ISO 15118-20 offers AC power, not current, and does not publish it.

DC welding detection polls until the reported voltage is strictly below 60 V. If voltage remains unsafe for 20 accepted responses or 10 seconds, Ev15118 logs and publishes welding_detection_failed with the response count as cycles and the last reported voltage as last_voltage_V, left out when no response was accepted, then attempts SessionStop with termination, even when a pause was requested. DIN has no pause field on the wire; a failed welding check prevents retaining its session for resume. ISO 15118-20 closes both safe and failed checks with Processing Finished, then sends SessionStop after its response. Each request waits for the configured message timeout or the protocol’s message timeout when none is configured. The welding deadline does not extend an outstanding request’s response deadline. A safe answer received after the welding deadline still counts as safe and preserves a requested pause. A missing response ends the session without SessionStop. In ISO 15118-2 and DIN, it also reports welding failure. In ISO 15118-20, it reports welding failure only before the Processing Finished request. A missing response to that request leaves the settled result unchanged: a safe check reports no failure, and a failed check is reported only once.
